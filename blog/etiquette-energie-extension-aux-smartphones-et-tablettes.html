Energy Label: Extension to Smartphones and Tablets from 20 June 2025

Habbine Estelle KimJun 26, 2025

Originally confined to household appliances (refrigerators, dishwashers, ovens, range hoods, tumble dryers, lamps, light fittings, space heaters, air conditioners, washing machines and televisions), the energy label becomes mandatory from 20 June 2025 for all new smartphones and tablets placed on the market in the European Union.

This measure stems from European Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1670, which entered into force on 20 September 2023 and applies from 20 June 2025. It forms part of the European Green Deal and the transition towards a circular economy.

Extending the energy label to smartphones and tablets is part of a broader European strategy to promote more responsible consumption and a more sustainable industry. The change pursues several key objectives:

✅ Increasing transparency for consumers by providing them with clear, comparable and reliable information on the energy performance and reparability of products;

🌱 Strengthening ecodesign requirements by encouraging manufacturers to build durability, ease of disassembly and energy efficiency criteria into products from the design stage;

⚙️ Stimulating technical innovation by encouraging progress in battery life, robustness, consumption and reparability;

💡 Reducing energy consumption across Europe, with expected positive effects on environmental impact and energy bills.

🧾 What is the energy label?

The energy label is a standardised sheet summarising a product’s characteristics in terms of:

  • energy consumption (kWh/year);
  • efficiency (rated from A to G);
  • reparability;
  • endurance per battery charge cycle, in hours and minutes;
  • resistance to accidental drops; and
  • protection against dust and water.
  • It also includes a QR code linking to a detailed product information sheet in the European online database EPREL (European Product Registry for Energy Labelling).
  • 📌 Important: this obligation applies only to new products. Second-hand devices fall outside its scope.

The energy label contains a core set of mandatory information common to all electrical household products:

  • Manufacturer’s name and model reference;
  • Energy class: shown by a colour code and a letter from A to G (or from A+++ to D for older models still in circulation);
  • Annual energy consumption, expressed in kilowatt-hours (kWh).

together with information specific to the product category concerned:

  • For a washing machine or dishwasher: annual water consumption (in litres);
  • For a refrigerator or washing machine: noise level (in decibels – dB);
  • For a television: screen size (in centimetres – cm).
  • Products covered:
  • All smartphones and tablets placed on the EU market as new products from 20 June 2025.

❌ Excluded:

  • Foldable smartphones: fitted with a flexible screen designed to be folded without breaking.
  • High-security smartphones: dedicated to specific professional uses involving high-security communications.
  • The Regulation provides for a review of these exclusions by 20 September 2027 at the latest, including the possibility of bringing foldable smartphones within the scheme at that point.

⚡Calculating the energy efficiency index

The energy efficiency index (EEI) measures a device’s endurance relative to its energy capacity. It is used to determine the energy class shown on the new energy label for smartphones and tablets.

🧮 Step 1: Battery endurance (ENDdevice)

Device endurance is the length of time (in hours) for which the smartphone or tablet operates under a standardised test scenario.

ENDdevice=ENDtest\text{ENDdevice} = \text{ENDtest}ENDdevice=ENDtest

Where ENDtest = test duration in hours (rounded to two decimal places).

🧮 Step 2: Calculating the EEI

The EEI is calculated using the following formula:

  • EEI = Energy efficiency index (in 1/W)
  • ENDDevice: test duration
  • Unom = Nominal voltage of the device (in volts)
  • Crated = Rated battery capacity (in mAh)
  • The higher the EEI, the more energy-efficient the smartphone is relative to its battery capacity.
  • Mandatory information

The following information will now appear on smartphone and tablet labels:

  • Annual electricity consumption (in kWh)
  • Energy efficiency class (from A to G)
  • Reparability index
  • Battery endurance
  • Level of dust and water protection
  • Resistance to accidental drops
  • QR code linking to the European product information sheet (EPREL database)

🔧 A closer look at the reparability index

The reparability index is the most technical component of the energy label. Rated from A (very good) to E (poor), it is intended to reflect how easily a device can be repaired, a criterion that is increasingly central to European ecodesign policy.

The reparability index of a smartphone or tablet is based on an assessment of how easily several essential components of the device can be accessed, disassembled and replaced.

Legal implications for businesses

For manufacturers:

  • Update packaging, product documentation and web interfaces before placing products on the market.
  • Enter the technical data in the EPREL database.

For distributors:

  • Display the label visibly at physical points of sale and on e-commerce websites.
  • Clearly inform consumers of what the classes and indices mean.

👥 What does it mean for consumers?

The energy label encourages responsible purchasing by providing objective benchmarks on the durability, battery life and robustness of devices.
