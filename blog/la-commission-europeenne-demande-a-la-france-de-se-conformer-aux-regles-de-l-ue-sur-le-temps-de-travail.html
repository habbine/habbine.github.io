The European Commission Calls on France to Comply with EU Working Time Rules

Habbine Estelle KimAug 7, 2025

The European Commission recently opened infringement proceedings against France by sending it a letter of formal notice (reference: INFR(2025)4012) for failure to comply with EU rules on working time, in particular Directive 2003/88/EC.

In France, no specific provision governs what happens to paid leave when an employee falls ill during a period of annual leave that has already begun. French law does not grant a right to recover paid leave for periods of incapacity for work, except in the case of an accident at work or an occupational disease. The European Commission, however, considers this situation to be contrary to the EU Working Time Directive. In its view, employees who fall ill during their annual leave should be able to recover the days of leave lost because of their illness.

France now has two months to respond to this letter of formal notice and take the necessary measures to remedy the shortcomings identified by the Commission. Failing a satisfactory response within that period, the Commission may move to the next stage of the infringement procedure by issuing a reasoned opinion against France.

EU law

Directive 2003/88/EC regulates working conditions by guaranteeing, among other things, a number of rights relating to working time and to the health and safety of workers in the European Union.

According to the European Commission’s publication of 18 June 2025 entitled “June infringements package: key decisions”:

“The Commission considers that French legislation does not ensure that workers who fall ill during their annual leave can subsequently recover the days of annual leave that coincided with their illness. The Commission therefore considers that French legislation does not comply with the Working Time Directive and does not safeguard workers’ health and safety. (...)”

https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/fr/inf…

The Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) has indeed held, with regard to Working Time Directive 2003/88/EC, that an employee who falls ill during their period of annual leave retains their entitlement to paid leave (CJEU, 21 June 2012, Case C-78/11, ANGED).

Along the same lines, the CJEU, when asked to interpret Article 7 of the Directive, made clear in a judgment that this provision draws no distinction according to the origin of the absence due to sickness (CJEU, Case C-520/06, 20 January 2009; CJEU, Grand Chamber, 24 January 2012, Dominguez, C-282/10).

French law: case law

The French Supreme Court (Cour de cassation) holds that:

“while an employee who falls ill during their paid leave cannot require to take at a later date the leave of which they were unable to benefit because of their sick leave, the employer having discharged its obligation towards them, an employee whose employment contract is already suspended by sick leave on the leave departure date set by the employer retains their entitlement to leave and may request to take it at a later date, so that the employer, which has not been released from its obligation, remains bound to allow them to exercise that right for the portion of leave not taken because of the sick leave, where the latter ends before the close of the paid leave period” (Cass. soc., 4 December 1996, No. 93-44.907).
“having regard to the purpose assigned to paid annual leave by Council Directive 93/104/EC of 23 November 1993 concerning certain aspects of the organisation of working time, where an employee has been unable to take their paid annual leave during the year provided for by the Labour Code or a collective agreement because of absences linked to an accident at work or an occupational disease, the paid leave accrued must be carried over until after the date on which the employee returns to work” (Cass. soc., 27 September 2007, 05-42.293). This position has been maintained in interpreting Directive 2003/88/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 4 November 2003 (Cass. soc., 15 September 2021, 20-16.010).

Carried-over leave entitlements and accrued leave entitlements are of the same nature, so the rules for setting the order of departures on annual leave apply to carried-over annual leave; i.e. an employer cannot compel an employee to take all of their carried-over paid leave without giving sufficient notice (Cass. soc., 8-7-20, No. 18-21681).

French law: the Labour Code

Article L3141-3 of the Labour Code (Code du travail) provides that an employee accrues 2.5 working days (jours ouvrables) of paid leave per month of actual work with the same employer, up to a total ceiling of thirty (30) working days per year.

Under Article L3141-1 of the Labour Code, every employee is entitled each year to paid leave payable by the employer.

Article L3141-12 of the Labour Code provides that leave may be taken from the date of hiring, without prejudice to the rules for determining the leave period and the order of departures and the rules for splitting leave laid down under the conditions provided for in that section.
