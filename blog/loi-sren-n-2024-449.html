SREN Act (Law No. 2024-449)

Habbine Estelle KimJul 8, 2024

Digital · Legislation · DSA · DGA · Protection · Minors · Personal data · Cybersecurity · Digital platform · Cloud

Law No. 2024-449 of 21 May 2024 (JORF No. 0117 of 22 May 2024) to secure and regulate the digital space (the “SREN” Act, loi visant à sécuriser et réguler l’espace numérique) implements the EU regulations forming part of the European digital package.

(JORF No. 0117 of 22 May 2024; Regulation (EU) 2022/868 (Data Governance Act or “DGA”); Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (Digital Services Act or “DSA”); Regulation (EU) 2022/1925 (Digital Markets Act or “DMA”); Conseil constitutionnel (French Constitutional Council) Decision No. 2024-866 DC of 17 May 2024)

Digital regulators such as the CNIL (French data protection authority), ARCOM, ARCEP and the DGCCRF are required to coordinate their protection efforts in implementing these texts.

The SREN Act contributes to creating a safer, more transparent online environment that respects users’ rights, with a view to strengthening digital trust.

Its main objectives include:

  • strengthening users’ online safety;
  • strengthening the protection of minors and citizens online;
  • combating disinformation and illegal content online;
  • regulating digital assets;
  • strengthening users’ protection against cyber threats (e.g. cyberattacks, hacking and online fraud);
  • strengthening businesses’ obligations regarding the confidentiality and security of personal data;
  • combating terrorist content and child sexual abuse material;
  • developing the economy of games with monetisable digital objects (jeux à objets numériques monétisables) and the cloud computing market;
  • strengthening trust and competition in the data economy.

The SREN Act provides for several measures, including the following:

  • Extension of the territorial scope of Law No. 78-17 of 6 January 1978 (the French Data Protection Act, loi informatique et libertés): the SREN Act applies to the processing of personal data by a controller or processor established outside the European Union where that processing relates to the monitoring of the behaviour of individuals within the European Union.
  • Obligations on very large online platform services regarding moderation, confidentiality of communications, transparency, risk awareness and training, with a view to the safety and quality of online content
  • Prohibition on displaying advertising based on profiling
  • Obligations on operators of games with monetisable digital objects to ensure the integrity, reliability and transparency of gaming operations and the protection of minors; to prohibit minors from playing; to verify the player’s majority and identity in advance; and to prevent excessive or pathological gaming, fraudulent or criminal activities, money laundering and terrorist financing.
  • Online protection of minors: the Audiovisual and Digital Communication Regulatory Authority (Autorité de régulation de la communication audiovisuelle et numérique, ARCOM) has extended powers. It must now draw up and publish, after obtaining the opinion of the National Commission on Informatics and Liberty (Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés, CNIL), a framework setting the minimum technical requirements applicable to age verification systems. ARCOM may require publishers and service providers to carry out an audit of the compliance of these systems. After formal notice and ex post review by the administrative court, ARCOM may also order the blocking or delisting of pornographic websites that do not verify their users’ age. ARCOM may also impose, after obtaining the CNIL’s opinion, a penalty not exceeding EUR 150,000 or 2% of worldwide turnover excluding tax for the previous financial year, whichever is higher. This maximum rises to EUR 300,000 or 4% of worldwide turnover excluding tax, whichever is higher, where the breach is repeated within five years of the date on which the first penalty became final.
  • Criminal penalties: hate speech, cyberbullying, child sexual abuse material and other serious offences are punishable by penalties such as a ban from social networks for 6 months, or one year for repeat offences, for several specifically listed offences (Article 131-35-1, I, of the Criminal Code). Article 226-8 of the Criminal Code has been amended to specifically cover deepfakes (Article 226-8-1). In addition, failure to remove child sexual abuse material immediately, within 24 hours of a request from ARCOM, is a criminal offence under Article 6-1-1.-I. of the Law of 21 June 2004, punishable by one year’s imprisonment and a fine of EUR 250,000.
