Occupational Health and Safety: New Obligations and Medical Document Templates from 1 July 2025

Habbine Estelle KimJun 26, 2025

From 1 July 2025, several regulatory texts strengthen employers’ occupational health and safety obligations. The changes concern in particular:

  • the entry into force of new templates for the medical documents used to monitor employees’ health (fitness-for-work notices, unfitness notices, monitoring certificates, etc.);
  • the implementation of enhanced prevention measures to address extreme heat.

📌 New medical document templates

As part of monitoring employees’ health, occupational health professionals issue various documents, such as the monitoring certificate (attestation de suivi), recommendations for workstation adjustments, the fitness-for-work notice (avis d’aptitude) or, where applicable, the unfitness notice (avis d’inaptitude).

From 1 July 2025, new document templates will apply.

  • Key changes:

With the exception of the monitoring certificate, these templates now include two mandatory elements:

  • A tick box for the employee bearing the statement: “I acknowledge receipt of the notice/proposal dated [date]”; and
  • A space for the employee’s signature.
  • The monitoring certificate template will include a new tick box headed: “Immediate referral to the occupational physician”.

The list of medical visits that may give rise to its issuance will be extended to include, in particular:

  • the post-exposure visit,
  • the end-of-career (post-professional) visit,
  • and the mid-career visit.
  • Enhanced wording in the event of a post-exposure visit or redeployment, and in the event of medical unfitness.

The new unfitness notice template will state explicitly that exemption from the redeployment obligation is an “exceptional case” which deprives the employee of the right to be redeployed by the employer. It results in dismissal without consultation of the Social and Economic Committee (CSE) on any redeployment proposals.

These documents are governed by the French Labour Code (Articles L.4624-4 to L.4624-6), and use of the templates is now mandatory.

📄 New mandatory Cerfa form for paper sick notes

In 2024, the French national health insurance fund (Assurance Maladie) identified financial losses exceeding €30 million linked to fraudulent sick notes, compared with only €8 million in 2023. This sharp rise is directly linked to the sale of fake sick notes through social media and the internet, a worrying phenomenon that weakens the social protection system and undermines trust in medical sick leave certificates.

To combat such fraud, the Assurance Maladie has introduced a reinforced Cerfa sick leave notice form that is difficult to falsify and more secure. This paper form, now mandatory in certain situations (notably home visits), incorporates several innovative security features:

  • Special anti-counterfeiting paper;
  • A holographic label;
  • Magnetic ink;
  • Prescriber identification marks to authenticate the origin of the sick note.
  • These features are designed to guarantee the authenticity of sick notes and to protect both insured persons and employers.
  • The paper form can be ordered on amelipro.

The Assurance Maladie points out that submitting a false sick note, even through negligence (a copy, a scan, or use of an obsolete form), exposes the insured person to penalties such as:

  • Full repayment of daily allowances unduly received
  • A financial penalty of up to three times the amount of the loss suffered
  • Criminal prosecution in cases of proven fraud, in particular for forgery and use of forged documents
  • Prison sentences of up to 5 years
  • Fines of up to €375,000
  • ❗ Important: from 1 July 2025, any sick note issued on an old version of the form or as a copy (scan, PDF, etc.) will be rejected.

☀️ Heat risks: new obligations for employers

Decree No. 2025-482 of 27 May 2025 (décret en Conseil d’État) on the protection of workers against heat-related risks introduces new obligations for the prevention of heat-related risks. These provisions will apply from 1 July 2025 and concern all employers, regardless of company size or sector of activity.

The text aims to anticipate and limit the risks associated with extreme heat, particularly during weather alert periods (yellow, orange and red levels).

The identified risks include:

  • deterioration of working conditions (increased fatigue, reduced alertness),
  • workplace accidents (linked to loss of concentration or fainting),

physical symptoms such as:

  • fever;
  • migraines;
  • muscle cramps;
  • dehydration;
  • heatstroke.

Under labour regulations and the recommendations of the Health and Safety Department of the CDG 66 (Centre de gestion des Pyrénées-Orientales), employers must prioritise the following actions:

  • Reducing or eliminating exposure to heat at workstations
  • Adapting workstations and premises to limit ambient heat
  • Adjusting working hours and increasing rest periods to avoid heat-related risks
  • Putting in place effective technical means (fans, misting systems, insulation, blinds, shading)
  • Mandatory provision of sufficient fresh drinking water, particularly for outdoor work
  • Distribution of suitable personal protective equipment (PPE) against heat and solar radiation
  • Informing and training staff on the risks of extreme heat and good practices

Employers must step up the following measures during a heatwave or an episode of intense heat:

  • For outdoor staff, ensure that fresh drinking water, kept cool, is available in the immediate vicinity of the workstation
  • Where there is no running water on site (e.g. a construction site), provide at least 3 litres of water per person per day
  • Exercise heightened vigilance for vulnerable staff (older people, people who are ill, etc.)
  • Put in place enhanced reporting procedures for lone workers to prevent incidents

The Health and Safety Department recommends that employers be particularly vigilant once the ambient temperature exceeds 30 °C in the shade, and encourage staff to take the necessary precautions (wearing light clothing, taking regular breaks, drinking frequently).

🔔 The four alert levels

The Order (arrêté) of 27 May 2025 on the determination of heatwave alert thresholds under the specific Météo-France system sets out the various weather alert thresholds defined by Météo-France, which are now directly linked to companies’ prevention obligations in the face of extreme heat.

🟢 Green alert – seasonal monitoring: No immediate risk has been identified, but continuous monitoring is recommended.

🟡 Yellow alert – heat peak: Exposure for 1 to 2 days to intense heat, presenting a risk to workers’ health owing to their working conditions or physical activity.

🟠 Orange alert – heatwave: Intense and lasting heat, likely to cause a widespread health risk for the exposed population, including in the workplace.

🔴 Red alert – extreme heatwave, exceptional in its duration, intensity and geographical extent, with a major health impact or likely to cause serious disruption to activity (outages, service interruptions, etc.).

An “episode of intense heat” means any period during which the yellow, orange or red alert threshold is reached. It triggers the employer’s enhanced prevention obligations (adapting workstations, informing employees, access to water, etc.).

“Heatwave periods”, which give entitlement to compensation for work stoppages in the construction and public works (BTP) sector, are defined by the orange or red alert threshold being reached.

From 1 July 2025, companies will be required to implement prevention measures from the yellow alert threshold onwards, such as:

  • An assessment of heat-related risks from the yellow alert level,
  • The implementation of practical prevention measures: adjusted working hours, access to water, ventilation, etc.
  • An emergency plan to detect cases of illness and intervene quickly.
  • 🛡️ The DUERP (Document unique d’évaluation des risques professionnels, the single occupational risk assessment document) must be updated accordingly.

Why these changes in employment law?

These measures aim to:

  • Improve the traceability of work-related medical data,
  • Reduce administrative fraud,
  • Anticipate climate risks to employees’ health.
