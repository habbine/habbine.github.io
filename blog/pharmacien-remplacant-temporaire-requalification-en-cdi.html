Temporary Locum Pharmacist: Reclassification as a Permanent Contract (CDI)

Habbine Estelle KimMar 13, 2024

Employment law · Medical · Health · Unregistered pharmacist · Conditions · Replacement · Reclassification · Employment contract

🚨 An employer cannot rely on its own negligence to justify its own wrongdoing.

(Basse-Terre Court of Appeal, 11 January 2016, No. 13/01666)

In this case, an employee was hired by a pharmacy as a pharmacist on a fixed-term basis of less than one month, to replace the pharmacist who owned the pharmacy (pharmacien titulaire de l'officine).

On the nullity of the replacement contract

The employer claimed that there had never been a valid replacement contract (ab initio), because the employee was not entered on the register of any of the sections of the National Order of Pharmacists (Ordre national des pharmaciens).

According to the Court of Appeal, even though it was true that the employee was not entered on the register of any of the sections of the National Order of Pharmacists and did not meet the statutory conditions for replacing a pharmacist (Article R 5015-15 of the former Public Health Code (CSP)), it was for the employer to carry out the checks and formalities required prior to hiring.

The Court held that the employer could not rely on its own negligence to justify its own wrongdoing. Accordingly, the replacement contract could not be declared null and void.

On reclassification as a permanent contract

The employee sought reclassification of the employment relationship as a permanent contract (contrat à durée indéterminée, CDI) from the outset, on the ground that there was no written employment contract (Article 18 of the extended collective bargaining agreement for retail pharmacies, IDCC 1996).

The Court of Appeal granted that claim, together with the claim for a reclassification indemnity. It held that the provisions of Articles L.1242-1 et seq. of the Labour Code on fixed-term contracts (contrats à durée déterminée, CDD) were enacted to protect the employee. In other words, the employer cannot rely on their non-observance, that right being reserved to the employee. A fixed-term contract must comply not only with the formal and substantive conditions laid down by the Public Health Code, but also with the statutory formalities laid down by the Labour Code. In this case, the formalities completed by the employer did not meet the statutory or collective-agreement requirements.

On the termination of the employment contract

Given the reclassification of the contractual relationship as a permanent contract, the Court of Appeal held that the termination of the contract was governed by the dismissal rules of the French Labour Code, which the pharmacy had not complied with. The dismissal was held to be procedurally irregular and without real and serious cause (sans cause réelle et sérieuse).

Furthermore, employees with less than 2 years’ seniority are entitled, in the event of unfair dismissal, to compensation corresponding to the loss suffered (Article L.1235-5 of the Labour Code).

Finally, the Court of Appeal held that the bonus received by the employee at the end of the replacement remained the employee’s, notwithstanding the subsequent reclassification as a CDI.

On undeclared work

The employee criticised the employer for failing to file a pre-hiring declaration (déclaration préalable d'embauche), which constituted the offence of concealed employment (dissimulation d'emploi) (Article L.8221-5 of the Labour Code).

The Court of Appeal held that the intentional element was not established, since the employer had registered the employee with URSSAF (the social security contributions collection agency), albeit late, and had issued payslips for the period worked showing the overtime hours worked and paid.

Decision

⚠ The Court of Appeal ordered the pharmacy to pay the employee the sums due in respect of the reclassification indemnity, damages for unfair termination and costs under Article 700 of the Code of Civil Procedure.
