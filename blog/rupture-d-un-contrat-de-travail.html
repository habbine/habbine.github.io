Employment

Termination of an Employment Contract

Habbine Estelle KimAug 21, 2024

Case law · Employment law · Time limit · Formalities · Non-compete clause · Probationary period · Dismissal · Termination of an employment contract

The facts

An employee was hired as sales director by a financial holding company. He had a six-month probationary period, renewable once. He was also bound by a non-compete clause, any waiver of which had to be notified by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt within a maximum of 15 days following notification of the termination of the employment contract.

On 24 July 2015, his employer notified him by letter of the termination of his contract, after the probationary period had ended.

The employer then communicated its waiver of the non-compete clause by emails dated 4 and 6 August 2015.

The decision

The French Supreme Court (Cour de cassation) upheld the decision of the Court of Appeal (Cour d'appel).

  1. The termination letter must be treated as a dismissal without real and serious cause (licenciement sans cause réelle et sérieuse), without the trial judge being required to examine the reasons put forward by the employer.
  2. The formalities specified in the non-compete clause must be strictly complied with. Failing this, the employer will be deemed not to have validly waived the non-compete clause.

(Cass., 3 July 2024, appeal No. 22-17.452)
