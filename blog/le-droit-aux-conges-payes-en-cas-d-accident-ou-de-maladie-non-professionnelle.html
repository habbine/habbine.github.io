The Right to Paid Leave in the Event of a Non-Work-Related Accident or Illness

Habbine Estelle KimNov 12, 2024

Employment · Sick leave · Illness · Accident · Paid leave

Under the former French Labour Code (Code du travail), an employment contract suspended because of a non-work-related accident or illness did not allow the employee to accrue paid leave entitlements.

Article 37 of Law No. 2024-364 of 22 April 2024 laying down various provisions adapting French law to European Union law (DDADUE) has now codified the principles laid down in the three judgments delivered by the Social Chamber of the Cour de cassation (French Supreme Court) on 13 September 2023 (appeals Nos. 22-17.340 to 22-17.342, No. 22-17.638, No. 22-10.529 and No. 22-11.106).

In those judgments, the Court held that the right to paid leave is an essential principle of EU law, applicable to persons who qualify as “workers”, without any condition linked to actual work. It found that the application of Article L. 3141-3 of the Labour Code, which makes the accrual of paid leave conditional on the performance of actual work, should be partially disapplied. That conclusion rests on the principles of proportionality, effectiveness (effet utile) and the primacy of EU law:

  • “7. Under Article L. 3141-3 of the Labour Code, an employee is entitled to two and a half working days (jours ouvrables) of leave per month of actual work with the same employer.
  1. The right to paid annual leave is an essential principle of European Union social law (CJEU, judgment of 6 November 2018, Bauer and Willmeroth, C-569/16 and C-570/16, paragraph 80).
  2. It follows from the case law of the Court of Justice of the European Union that Directive 2003/88/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 4 November 2003 concerning certain aspects of the organisation of working time draws no distinction between workers who are absent from work on sick leave during the reference period and those who have actually worked during that period. It follows that, with regard to workers on duly prescribed sick leave, the right to paid annual leave conferred by that directive on all workers cannot be made subject by a Member State to a condition of having actually worked during the reference period laid down by that State (CJEU, judgment of 20 January 2009, Schultz-Hoff, C-350/06, paragraph 41; CJEU, judgment of 24 January 2012, Dominguez, C-282/10, paragraph 20).
  3. The Court of Justice of the European Union holds that it is for the national court to ascertain, taking the whole body of domestic law into consideration and applying the interpretative methods recognised by it, whether it can arrive at an interpretation of that law that ensures the full effectiveness of Article 7 of Directive 2003/88/EC and achieves an outcome consistent with the objective pursued by it (CJEU, judgment of 24 January 2012, Dominguez, C-282/10).
  4. In its judgment of 6 November 2018 (CJEU, judgment of 6 November 2018, Bauer and Willmeroth, C-569/16 and C- 570/16), the Court of Justice of the European Union held that where it is impossible to interpret national legislation in a manner that ensures its compliance with Article 7 of Directive 2003/88/EC and Article 31(2) of the Charter of Fundamental Rights, the national court must disapply that national legislation. (…)
  5. In the case of an employee whose employment contract is suspended as a result of sick leave for a non-work-related illness, the provisions of Article L. 3141-3 of the Labour Code, which make the right to paid leave conditional on the performance of actual work, do not allow an interpretation consistent with European Union law.
  6. Accordingly, since the dispute is between a holder of the right to leave and an employer who is a private individual, it is for the national court, within the limits of its jurisdiction, to ensure the legal protection deriving from Article 31(2) of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union and to guarantee its full effect by disapplying, where necessary, that national legislation.
  7. The application of Article L. 3141-3 of the Labour Code must therefore be partially disapplied insofar as it makes the accrual of paid leave entitlements by an employee whose employment contract is suspended as a result of sick leave for a non-work-related illness conditional on the performance of actual work, and it must be held that the employee may claim paid leave entitlements in respect of that period pursuant to Articles L. 3141-3 and L. 3141-9 of the Labour Code.
  8. The court of appeal, having rightly partially disapplied the provisions of domestic law contrary to Article 31(2) of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, correctly held that the employees had accrued paid leave entitlements during the suspension of their employment contracts on account of non-work-related illness.”

In this respect, the Cour de cassation’s press release of 13 September 2023 entitled “Paid leave and European Union law” states that:

  • “employees who are ill or injured will be entitled to paid leave for their period of absence, even if that absence is not linked to a workplace accident or an occupational disease;
  • in the event of a workplace accident, the calculation of paid leave entitlements will no longer be limited to the first year of sick leave;
  • the limitation period for the right to paid leave only starts to run once the employer has given the employee the opportunity to exercise that right in good time.”

Law No. 2024-364 of 22 April 2024 thus aims to bring French employment law into line with the requirements of European Union law on paid leave. It applies retroactively to periods between 1 December 2009 and 31 March 2024.

On 17 November 2023, the Cour de cassation referred to the Conseil constitutionnel (French Constitutional Council) a priority question of constitutionality (question prioritaire de constitutionnalité, QPC) concerning paragraph 5° of Article L. 3141-5 of the Labour Code.

Decision No. 2023-1079 of the Conseil constitutionnel of 8 February 2024, which found the provision constitutional, introduced a qualification:

“14. Secondly, under Article 6 of the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of 1789, the law ‘must be the same for all, whether it protects or punishes’. The principle of equality does not prevent the legislature from regulating different situations in different ways, nor from departing from equality for reasons of general interest, provided that, in either case, the resulting difference in treatment is directly related to the purpose of the law that establishes it.
  1. Occupational diseases and workplace accidents, which originate in the very performance of the employment contract, are distinct from other illnesses or accidents that may affect the employee.
  2. Thus, in view of the purpose of the law, the legislature was entitled to provide for different rules on the accrual of paid leave entitlements for employees on sick leave depending on the reason for the suspension of their employment contract.
  3. Accordingly, the difference in treatment resulting from the contested provisions, which is based on a difference in situation, is related to the purpose of the law.”

It follows that the difference in treatment between types of sick leave is justified, for the purpose of calculating paid leave entitlements, by the nature of the absence (sick leave for a work-related or a non-work-related accident or illness).

As a rule:

  • For each month of actual work with the same employer (Article L3141-3 of the Labour Code): the employee accrues 2.5 working days of paid leave per month worked (i.e. up to a maximum of 30 working days of paid leave for a full year of work during the reference period from 1 June of year N-1 to 31 May of year N, unless a collective bargaining agreement or a company-level collective agreement provides for a different period)
  • This calculation method applies whatever the type of employment contract (permanent contract (CDI), fixed-term contract (CDD), temporary agency work) and whatever its working time (full-time or part-time).

In the event of sick leave, the new Articles L3141-1 et seq. of the Labour Code provide that:

  • For an occupational disease or a workplace accident (Article L3141-5, paragraph 5°, of the Labour Code):

The employee accrues 2.5 working days of paid leave per month of absence, with no limit on the accrual period (i.e. up to a maximum of 30 working days for a full year of work during the reference period from 1 June of year N-1 to 31 May of year N, unless a collective bargaining agreement or a company-level collective agreement provides for a different period)

Periods of leave exceeding one year are therefore treated as actual working time.

  • For a non-occupational illness or an ordinary accident (Articles L3141-4, L3141-5, paragraph 7°, L3141-5-1 and L3141-6 of the Labour Code):

The employee accrues 2 working days of paid leave per month of absence (i.e. up to a maximum of 24 working days during the reference period from 1 June of year N-1 to 31 May of year N, unless a collective bargaining agreement or a company-level collective agreement provides for a different period).

The period of sick leave is thus treated as actual working time.

This ceiling is assessed taking into account the statutory and contractual leave days already accrued (seniority, dependent children, etc.).

The employer’s duty to inform (Art. L3141-19-3 of the Labour Code)

The employer is now required to inform the employee of the following:

  • The number of paid leave days available, whether or not they were accrued during periods of illness or workplace accident.
  • The deadline by which this leave may be taken.

This information must be provided:

  • By any means guaranteeing receipt by the employee: registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt (LRAR), letter delivered by hand against a signed receipt, email, or a statement on the payslip.
  • Within one month of the employee’s return to work.
  • After each period of sick leave.
  • and it determines when the carry-over period starts to run (except in the specific case set out below).
  • Carry-over period for taking paid leave (Art. L3141-19-1 and L3141-19-2 of the Labour Code)

An employee who was unable to take all or part of their paid leave during the scheduled period because of illness or accident has a carry-over period of 15 months in which to use it (unless a company-level agreement or, failing that, a sector-level collective agreement sets a longer carry-over period).

At the end of this 15-month period, the right to leave is definitively lost: the employee can neither use the leave nor claim a payment in lieu.

As a reminder, if the employment contract is terminated before the employee has been able to take all their paid leave, including carried-over leave (for example, in the event of resignation or dismissal for unfitness for work), the employer must pay the employee a payment in lieu of paid leave (indemnité compensatrice de congés payés).

Time limit for claiming paid leave in respect of sick leave issued before 24 April 2024:

  • For employees still in post: 2 years from the publication of the law, i.e. until 24 April 2026 (foreclosure period, forclusion)
  • For employees who have left the company (Article L. 3245-1 of the Labour Code): 3 years from the date of termination of the employment contract (limitation period, prescription).
