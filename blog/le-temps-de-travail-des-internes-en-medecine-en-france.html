Working Time of Medical Residents in France

Habbine Estelle KimJun 2, 2025

🏛️ Maximum working hours and service obligations of medical residents

The working time of medical residents (internes) in France is governed by Articles R.6153-2 et seq. of the Public Health Code (Code de la santé publique), Decree No. 2015-225 of 26 February 2015 and Instruction DGOS/RH4/2014/128 of 22 April 2014.

In addition, the interministerial instruction of 19 September 2024 addresses the service obligations and working time of third-cycle students in medicine, dentistry and pharmacy (DGOS/RH5/DGESIP/2024/101; see Bulletin officiel Santé – Protection sociale – Solidarité No. 2024/29 of 15 October 2024).

These texts set a maximum working time of 48 hours per week, calculated as an average over a 3-month period.

This ceiling includes:

  • Hours worked during placements;
  • On-site duty shifts (gardes) and on-call duties (astreintes), including call-outs and travel time;
  • The half-day of university training.

Compliance with working time rules is intended to ensure a reasonable balance between the demands of training and supervised professional practice, which is essential for gradual preparation for the full exercise of medical duties.

📅 Weekly allocation of service obligations

Residents’ service obligations are spread over ten half-days per week:

  • On placement: 8 half-days of actual service in the host facility, with a mandatory minimum break of 15 minutes per half-day.

Outside the placement: 2 half-days:

  • One half-day of training supervised by the specialty coordinator, counted as actual working time.
  • One half-day of personal time devoted to self-directed learning (writing the thesis, consolidating knowledge and skills), not counted as actual working time.

🌙 On-site duty, on-call duty and safety rest

  1. On-site duty shifts (gardes)

One night shift per week, equivalent to two half-days.

One 24-hour shift (Sunday or public holiday) per month, equivalent to four half-days.

Duty shifts start no earlier than 6:30 pm and end no earlier than 8:30 am the following morning.

A resident may not be required to work beyond 24 consecutive hours, save in exceptional circumstances to ensure continuity of care and where the duty roster cannot otherwise be filled.

(Order (arrêté) of 10 September 2002 on duty shifts of residents, medical residents and students appointed to temporarily fill a resident post, and on the introduction of safety rest; Order of 6 November 1995 on the organisation and remuneration of medical duty shifts performed by residents in public healthcare institutions)

  1. On-call duty (astreintes)

Organised mainly in university hospitals (centres hospitaliers universitaires).

The resident must remain available without being required to be continuously present on site.

On-call periods cover hours outside normal service, generally between 6:30 pm and 8:30 am, as well as Sundays and public holidays.

Where the resident is called out, travel and intervention time is counted as actual working time. Even where there is no call-out, on-call duty gives rise to a flat-rate allowance.

(Order of 6 August 2015 on on-call duties of residents)

  1. Safety rest (repos de sécurité)

A rest period of 11 consecutive hours must be taken immediately after the end of a night shift or half-night shift, and after the last call-out during an on-call period.

Any hospital, university or outpatient activity is prohibited during this rest.

The institution is responsible for ensuring that this rest is actually taken.

(Order of 10 September 2002 on duty shifts of residents, medical residents and students appointed to temporarily fill a resident post, and on the introduction of safety rest; Article R6153-2 of the Public Health Code)

📋 Planning and monitoring of working time

A provisional named duty roster must be drawn up every month, detailing:

  • Placement days.
  • On-site and on-call duties.
  • Training.
  • Safety rest periods.
  • Compensatory rest days.
  • This roster is drawn up by the practitioner in charge of the host unit, in liaison with the specialty coordinator, and approved by the director of the host facility.
  • Monthly and quarterly statements of the service obligations performed are made available to the resident and to the specialty coordinator.

(Article R6153-2-2 of the Public Health Code; Order of 30 June 2015 on the procedures for drawing up and transmitting duty rosters relating to residents’ working time)

📊 The reality on the ground: frequent overruns

Despite this regulatory framework, surveys by ISNI (the national union of medical residents) reveal significant overruns:

  • Residents work 59 hours per week on average.
  • 10% of residents report working more than 80 hours per week.

By specialty:

  • General practice: 50 hours per week on average.
  • Medical specialties (rheumatology, cardiology, geriatrics, neurology, etc.): 58 hours.
  • Surgical specialties: 75 hours.
  • These figures far exceed the statutory limit of 48 hours per week, highlighting a worrying gap between the rules and practice.

In a reasoned opinion of 28 March 2014, the European Commission had already found that France was not respecting several fundamental rights laid down in Directive 2003/88/EC in the organisation of residents’ working time (EC MEMO/14/241, 28 March 2014).

"The European Commission has asked France to respect the right of doctors in training to minimum rest periods and limited working hours (...) Currently, French legislation does not guarantee doctors in training several fundamental rights provided for by this directive, such as the limitation of average weekly working time to 48 hours. It does not take into account all the hours actually worked by doctors in training when applying the 48-hour maximum set by the Working Time Directive. Additional on-call periods and hours of university teaching are not covered, whereas the directive requires them to be considered as working time. Furthermore, French legislation does not allow working hours to be adequately monitored and makes it difficult to verify whether hospitals comply with the rules laid down in the directive. As a result, doctors in training often work excessive hours in French public hospitals. The request to France takes the form of a reasoned opinion under EU infringement procedures. (...) "

Penalties

Decree No. 2023-71 of 6 February 2023, published in the Journal officiel on 7 February 2023, introduces measures to strengthen the monitoring of working time by empowering the directors-general of regional health agencies (agences régionales de santé, ARS) to impose financial penalties on healthcare institutions where breaches of the rules on residents’ working time persist or worsen.

The procedure provides for formal notice (mise en demeure) to the institution concerned, followed by an adversarial phase. The financial penalty, determined by the director-general of the ARS, takes into account the seriousness, duration and repetition of the breaches, as well as the number of students concerned. Its amount may not exceed the total funding allocated for the third-cycle student posts hosted at the placement site concerned.
