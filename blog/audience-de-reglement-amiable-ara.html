Amicable Settlement Hearing (ARA)

Habbine Estelle KimDec 11, 2024

When a dispute arises, the parties may bring it before the State courts. In that case, the judge’s role is to “decide the dispute in accordance with the rules of law applicable to it” (Article 12 of the French Code of Civil Procedure, CPC).

However, there are also alternative or amicable methods of dispute resolution, such as negotiation, settlement agreements (transaction), conciliation, mediation, participatory procedure (procédure participative) and collaborative law.

Decree No. 2023-686 of 29 July 2023 on measures promoting the amicable settlement of disputes before the judicial court (tribunal judiciaire) introduced the amicable settlement hearing (audience de règlement amiable, “ARA”) at Articles 774-1 to 774-4 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

Decree No. 2024-673 of 3 July 2024 on various measures simplifying civil procedure and relating to regulated professions (known as “Magicobus I”), which entered into force on 1 September 2024, extends the scope of the ARA. These new rules apply to proceedings already pending on that date (Article 17 I of Decree 2024-673).

The following may now order that the parties be summoned to an ARA as provided for in Articles 774-1 to 774-4:

  • the trial bench of the commercial court (tribunal de commerce) (Article 860-2, paragraph 2, CPC)
  • the judge in charge of preparing the case (Article 863, paragraph 2, CPC)
  • the president of the commercial court hearing summary proceedings (référé) (Articles 873-1 and 873-2 CPC)
  • the president of the judicial court or the protection litigation judge (juge des contentieux de la protection) ruling in summary proceedings (Article 836-2 CPC)
  • the pre-trial judge (juge de la mise en état) (Article 785, paragraph 4, CPC)
  • the trial judge hearing a dispute concerning rights that the parties are free to dispose of (Articles 774-1 and 803 CPC; in commercial matters, Article R145-29-1 of the Commercial Code)
  • the president of the judicial court, sitting as commercial rents judge (juge des loyers commerciaux) (Article R145-29-1 of the Commercial Code)
  • the president of the commercial chamber ruling in summary proceedings in the départements of Bas-Rhin, Haut-Rhin and Moselle (ANNEX, Article 39 CPC)
  • the pre-trial judge (Article 803, paragraph 4, CPC)
  • These decisions, recorded by a note on the case file, are measures of judicial administration. They do not divest the judge hearing a dispute concerning rights that the parties are free to dispose of.
  • The ARA judge is designated by the court’s assignment order (ordonnance de roulement) issued before the start of the judicial year (Article L.121-3 of the Code of Judicial Organisation, COJ).
  • The ARA procedure is set out in Article 774-3 of the Code of Civil Procedure.
  • The judge hearing the dispute may decide to summon the parties to an ARA at any stage of the proceedings, either at the request of one of the parties or of the judge’s own motion after hearing their views.

The judge hearing the dispute must take into account the time needed to schedule an ARA within that court and ensure that referral to an ARA is not likely to lengthen the proceedings excessively.

Where the appropriateness of an ARA becomes apparent after the close of the pre-trial phase, the closing order (ordonnance de clôture) may be revoked, after the parties’ views have been sought, by the pre-trial judge or, once the hearing on the merits has begun, by the court.

The parties’ views are sought in accordance with the rules governing the procedure applicable to the dispute: written views in written procedure (pre-trial bulletin, bulletin de mise en état); views noted on the case file or recorded in minutes (Article 836-1 CPC for summary proceedings).

No particular formality governs the decision to refer a case to an ARA, which may take the form of a simple note on the case file.

The ARA does not divest the judge hearing the dispute.

It interrupts the proceedings and interrupts the time limit for lapse of the proceedings (péremption d’instance).

Where the proceedings have been interrupted by the decision to summon the parties to an ARA, a new time limit runs from the first hearing subsequently set before the judge hearing the case (Articles 369 and 392 CPC).

The summons is issued by any means, on the initiative of the registry of the bench seised, for a specified date.

The parties must appear in person, with assistance (Articles 762 and 763 CPC) or without (Article 761 CPC), where they are exempt from mandatory representation. Otherwise, they appear assisted by their lawyer.

The hearing is held in chambers (chambre du conseil), without the registry being present, in the manner determined by the judge in charge of the ARA.

Its length is not fixed and will vary depending on the nature of the case. The Circular of 17 October 2023 (NOR No. JUSC2324682C; CIRC No. CIV/06/23; REF No. C3/202330001121) recommends that it should not exceed one day.

Nothing prevents the case from being adjourned to a second ARA hearing.

The judge in charge of the ARA may bring it to an end at any time.

That decision is a measure of judicial administration and does not divest the court seised, but it does interrupt the proceedings.

The ARA is conducted by a judge other than the one handling the dispute. The referring judge must ensure that the case is directed to a hearing that is not too distant, so as to avoid delays, and must check that the date suits the parties and their lawyers (implementing circular of 17 October 2023).

At the end of the hearing, the parties may ask the judge in charge of the ARA, assisted by the court clerk, to record their full or partial agreement (Articles 130 and 131, paragraph 1, CPC).

The judge informs the judge hearing the dispute that the ARA has ended and, where applicable, forwards the minutes of agreement (Article 774-4 CPC).

The powers of the ARA judge are set out in Article 774-2 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

It should be borne in mind that the ARA aims at the amicable resolution of the dispute between the parties through a balanced exchange of their points of view, an assessment of their respective needs, positions and interests, and an understanding of the legal principles applicable to the dispute. To that end, the ARA judge:

  • may read the submissions and exhibits exchanged by the parties;
  • may carry out any findings, assessments, evaluations or reconstructions that the judge considers necessary, visiting the site if required;
  • determines the conditions under which the hearing is held;
  • may decide to hear the parties separately.
  • The ARA judge may not order an expert assessment, although one may nevertheless be commissioned by lawyers’ deed (acte d’avocats) (Article 1546-1 CPC).

The ARA is subject to the principle of confidentiality, unless the parties agree otherwise or in the following exceptional situations:

  • a) where there are overriding reasons of public policy or grounds relating to the protection of the best interests of the child or to the physical or psychological integrity of a person;
  • b) where disclosure of the existence or content of the resulting agreement is necessary for its implementation or enforcement (Article 774-3 CPC).

Following the Magicobus I Decree, the Ministry of Justice (Directorate of Civil Affairs and the Seal) issued a Circular, published on 19 July 2024 in the Official Bulletin of the Ministry of Justice (NOR: JUSC2419834C).
