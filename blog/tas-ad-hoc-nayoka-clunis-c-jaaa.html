CAS ad hoc – Nayoka Clunis v. Jamaica Athletics Administrative Association

Habbine Estelle KimJul 26, 2024

Ms Nayoka Clunis is a Jamaican hammer thrower.

The facts

On 7 July 2024, World Athletics (WA) published the final list of athletes certified to compete at the Paris 2024 Olympic Games. To the athlete’s great surprise, her name (although she had duly qualified for the Paris 2024 Olympic Games) did not appear on it. The Jamaica Athletics Administrative Association (JAAA) had failed to submit her name owing to an administrative error and the impact of Hurricane Beryl.

On 8 July 2024, the Director of the WA Competitions and Events Department replied to the President of the JAAA that WA could not grant the request to include the athlete’s name, because the 32 places in the hammer throw had been filled through the reallocation of 5 July 2024, in accordance with the qualification system. The best solution that could be offered to the athlete was to include her in the event of the withdrawal or cancellation of another athlete, in accordance with the International Olympic Committee (IOC) late athlete replacement policy.

By way of reminder, the final entry list of athletes competing at the Paris 2024 Olympic Games had to be sent to the IOC by 11.59 pm on 8 July 2024.

On 15 July 2024, the President of the JAAA wrote to the Director of the World Athletics (WA) Competitions and Events Department, pointing out that the situation had been caused by Hurricane Beryl. He reiterated his request that the athlete be included in the list of participants, even in the absence of any withdrawal or cancellation by another athlete.

On 18 July 2024, Ms Clunis (“the Applicant”) filed her application with the ad hoc Division of the Court of Arbitration for Sport (TAS/CAS) against the JAAA (“the Respondent”), with WA, the Jamaica Olympic Association (JOA), the IOC, Iryna Klymets and the Ukrainian Athletic Association (UAA) as “Interested Parties” (CAS OG 24/01).

The decision

In this case, it was not disputed that the Applicant’s name had not been entered in the registration system owing to an administrative error.

However, a procedural question arose: did the dispute arise within the ten (10) days preceding the Opening Ceremony of the Paris 2024 Olympic Games?

It was held that the date of 15 July 2024 did not fall within the temporal jurisdiction of the ad hoc Division of the Court of Arbitration for Sport (TAS/CAS).

Although the injustice suffered by the athlete was recognised and accepted, it was determined that the CAS ad hoc Division had no jurisdiction to hear the application under the Arbitration Rules for the Olympic Games.

This is because the dispute arose before the ten (10)-day period preceding the Opening Ceremony of the Paris 2024 Olympic Games.
