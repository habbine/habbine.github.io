Repayment of Unlawful Differential Bonuses: the Case of the Foreign-Trained PADHUE Doctors

Habbine Estelle KimJul 21, 2025

Since June 2025, nearly fifty foreign doctors who qualified outside the European Union (praticiens à diplôme hors Union européenne, PADHUE), recruited by the Grand Hôpital de l’Est francilien (GHEF), an institution formed by the merger of the hospitals of Meaux, Coulommiers and Marne-la-Vallée in Seine-et-Marne, have been ordered to repay so-called "differential" bonuses (primes différentielles) of up to €100,000, to be repaid in instalments over two years. These bonuses, paid monthly for several years and stipulated in the employment contracts as well as shown on the payslips, have been withdrawn retroactively by the new management. Management now considers that these bonuses or financial benefits had no basis in any regulatory text, and justifies this step by the need to respond to a claim from the Public Treasury (Trésor public) concerning a total of €2.7 million.

In response to this decision, several of the foreign doctors have brought actions before the Administrative Court (tribunal administratif) seeking the annulment of the order to repay the bonuses received.

They argue that there has been a unilateral breach of the employment contract, carried out without any explicit regulatory basis, as well as a manifest infringement of legal certainty. These challenges highlight not only the contractual fragility of practitioners who qualified outside the EU (PADHUE), but also the systemic consequences of the situation. Operationally, the measure is placing an additional burden on local hospitals, already weakened by the crisis in medical demographics. Institutionally, it is helping to create a damaging climate for the attractiveness of hospital careers, deterring foreign talent from joining the public service.

The claimants also point to the major personal and professional consequences of this unilateral and retroactive removal of an essential contractual clause. The doctors concerned report a sudden financial collapse (a loss of income that can exceed €2,000 a month), psychological suffering (burn-out, prolonged sick leave), and multiple resignations that directly threaten the functioning (and survival) of certain hospital departments. In the long run, this instability could accelerate the spread of local medical deserts, further undermining access to care in areas that are already under-served.

These actions could therefore raise several central questions of hospital public law: the lawfulness of the retroactive removal of contractual bonuses, respect for vested rights (droits acquis), and the liability of the administration in the event of a manifest error.

NB: Attractiveness bonuses (primes d’attractivité) are commonly used by hospitals to attract doctors to areas facing shortages, often described as medical deserts.

The shortage of practitioners remains a well-documented reality, acknowledged by Prime Minister François Bayrou himself. He ruled out any coercive measure regarding doctors’ freedom to choose where to practise, as part of the "Pact to combat medical deserts" (Pacte de lutte contre les déserts médicaux) presented on 25 April 2025. The plan was unveiled alongside several members of the government: Catherine Vautrin (Health, Labour, Solidarity and Families), Yannick Neuder (Health), Philippe Baptiste (Higher Education and Research) and Françoise Gatel (Rural Affairs).

In this strategic text, the government chose to introduce a principle of territorial solidarity among doctors rather than restrict their freedom of establishment, which is regarded as a fundamental right in private practice.

This stance marks a clear rejection of the cross-party bill championed by Socialist deputy Guillaume Garot, adopted by the National Assembly (Assemblée nationale) on 2 April 2025, which provided for prior authorisation by the Regional Health Agencies (ARS) for any new medical practice in certain over-served areas.

By contrast, the government plan is more closely aligned with the positions of the Senate, and in particular with Senator Philippe Mouiller, author of a more liberal counter-proposal supported by Les Républicains senators.
